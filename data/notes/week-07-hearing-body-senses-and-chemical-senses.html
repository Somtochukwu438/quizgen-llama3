Original Online Lecture

Auditory transduction

Sound moves the tympanic membrane and ossicles, creating waves in cochlear fluid. Basilar-membrane motion bends hair cells, converting mechanical energy into neural activity.

Check your understanding: Explain this concept in your own words and connect it to one earlier course concept.

Pitch and localization

Place coding uses maximal activity along the basilar membrane; timing information also contributes at lower frequencies. Interaural timing and intensity differences aid localization.

Body and pain

Somatosensory receptors signal touch, temperature, body position, and tissue threat. Pain reflects nociception plus spinal, brain, cognitive, emotional, and social modulation.

Check your understanding: Explain this concept in your own words and connect it to one earlier course concept.

Taste and smell

Taste receptors respond to several chemical qualities; olfactory receptors use combinatorial patterns. Flavor depends on integration of taste, smell, texture, temperature, and expectation.
